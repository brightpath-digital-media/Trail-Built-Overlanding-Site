FAQ

What is the difference between GMRS and FRS frequencies?

GMRS (General Mobile Radio Service) requires an FCC license and typically offers more channels and greater potential range than FRS (Family Radio Service), which is license-free and intended for short-range consumer use. Users who need extended range and additional channels should account for licensing requirements before choosing GMRS.

Do I need a license to use these radios?

Licensing depends on the radio type and frequency: GMRS radios require an FCC license, while FRS radios do not. Consult the FCC and local regulations for specific requirements prior to operation.

Two‑way radios can be a useful component of an emergency communication plan when used with appropriate planning and equipment. Evaluate radios for adequate range, channel availability, battery life and spare power options, and compliance with local licensing or operation rules. Include redundancies such as multiple radio types, standardized call procedures, regular battery maintenance, and integration with other emergency methods (cellular, satellite) for more reliable communication in varied terrain.

Two-way radios can serve as part of an emergency communication plan when used correctly. Recommended practices include programming important channels (for example, emergency services and NOAA weather radio) and keeping a readily accessible list of contacts and frequencies.
